# Shopify Niche Research

A niche is a specific audience with a shared identity, problem, or interest. Picking the right niche is a bigger lever than picking the right product — a great product in a bad niche dies; a mediocre product in a passionate niche can thrive.

## Niche vs. Sub-niche vs. Micro-niche

- **Niche**: "Fitness" — too broad, can't differentiate
- **Sub-niche**: "Home workout equipment" — manageable
- **Micro-niche**: "Resistance band training for postpartum moms" — this is where you win

**Always go micro.** A micro-niche is where you can be the obvious choice for a specific person.

## The 5 criteria for a viable niche

1. **Passionate audience**: Do people tattoo the logo/lifestyle? (e.g., motorcyclists, CrossFit, bass fishing, anime.) Passion = repeat purchases + tolerance for higher prices.

2. **Evergreen OR growing**: Google Trends stable or rising over 5 years. Avoid fads (fidget spinners, flammable hoverboards) unless you're entering deliberately early.

3. **Problem-rich**: The audience actively buys solutions. Forums, subreddits, and Facebook groups should be full of "does anyone know how to fix X" posts.

4. **Accessible ad targeting**: Can you reach them on Meta/TikTok/Google? Interest targeting exists (Meta: "CrossFit", "Vanlife"); or there are adjacent interests that proxy well.

5. **Profitable product range**: Products in the $30-150 range with 3x+ markup potential. Too cheap = ads unprofitable; too expensive = trust barrier.

## Research methodology

### Step 1: List 20 passions you or someone you know has

Your niche doesn't need to be yours, but you must understand the audience. Sources:
- Your own hobbies, communities, subcultures
- Your parents', partner's, friends' hobbies
- Reddit's list of subreddits with > 100K subscribers
- Facebook Groups with > 50K members
- YouTube channels with > 500K subs in a specific vertical

### Step 2: Audience size check

For each candidate, measure:

```
Google keyword volume (Ahrefs/SEMrush/free Google Keyword Planner):
  "[niche] gear", "[niche] accessories", "best [niche] products"

Facebook audience size (Meta Ads Manager → Audience estimate):
  Interest: "[niche]" → Potential reach
  Target: 500K - 50M for a viable niche

Subreddit subscribers: > 100K
Facebook group members (top 3 groups combined): > 200K
```

If an audience is < 500K addressable, scaling past $1K/day will be hard.

### Step 3: Competition analysis

Search the niche on:

- **Google** for `"[niche] store"` and `"shop [niche]"` — top 10 results = your competitors
- **Instagram** for niche hashtags → find stores, look at follower count + engagement
- **SimilarWeb** (free tier) — plug in competitor domains → traffic, top sources, top pages
- **Built With** → see what Shopify theme/apps they use
- **Ahrefs Site Explorer** (paid) → their top pages, traffic estimate, backlinks

Ideal niche: 3-10 established competitors (proves demand), but none dominate (room to enter). Avoid: 1 player owning 80% (Warby Parker in eyewear, Dollar Shave Club in razors — you can't unseat).

### Step 4: Problem-rich check

Go to:
- Reddit: top 50 posts of all time + top 50 of this month in the main subreddit. What problems keep recurring?
- Amazon 1-3 star reviews on top niche products. Complaints = product opportunities.
- Quora: top niche questions by views.

Write down 20 real problems. If you can't find 20, the niche is shallow.

### Step 5: AOV + LTV model

```
Avg order value (AOV) target: $50-100 for cold-traffic viability
Gross margin: ≥ 60%
Repeat purchase: does the niche buy > 2x/year?

Back-of-napkin:
  AOV $60 × Margin 65% = $39 gross profit/order
  Allow $20 CAC → Contribution margin $19/order
  4 orders/customer/year → $76 annual contribution per customer
```

If those numbers don't work, the niche isn't financially viable for paid ads.

## Niche archetypes that work

| Archetype | Why it works | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity-driven** | Tribal signaling, repeat buys | Vanlife, preppers, yoga moms, motorcycle clubs |
| **Hobby obsession** | High willingness to spend, gear-focused | Fly fishing, mechanical keyboards, birdwatching |
| **Profession-specific** | B2B-ish, deductible, high AOV | Nurses, real estate agents, chefs, plumbers |
| **Life transition** | Urgent, time-bounded spend | New parents, newlyweds, new homeowners, retirees |
| **Problem-specific** | Willing to pay to solve | Back pain, snoring, anxiety, dry skin |
| **Pet parents** | Emotional, recurring spend | Dog moms, cat owners, exotic pet keepers |

## Niches to approach with caution

- **Supplements & health claims** — Meta restrictions, FDA legal risk
- **Weight loss** — banned on most ad platforms
- **CBD / kratom / nicotine** — restricted payment processors + ads
- **Firearms & accessories** — platform bans
- **Children's products** — recall + liability risk
- **Fashion/apparel** — brutal returns, sizing, copycat competition

## Differentiation angles within a chosen niche

Once the niche is set, pick your angle:

1. **Demographic**: products for [sub-audience] — e.g., "golf apparel for women under 35"
2. **Price point**: budget vs. premium — e.g., "$150 premium cat trees" vs. the Walmart crowd
3. **Aesthetic**: minimalist, maximalist, retro, cottagecore — huge in home + fashion
4. **Values**: eco-friendly, vegan, made-in-USA, family-owned
5. **Convenience**: subscription, curation, bundles
6. **Expertise**: "Founded by a physical therapist" gives authority in health
7. **Community**: own the Discord/Facebook group, store is secondary to community

## AI-assisted research prompt

```
I'm looking for profitable Shopify niches. Given my background/interests in [X, Y, Z],
suggest 10 micro-niches that meet these criteria:
- Passionate audience of 500K-20M on Meta
- Evergreen (stable demand over 5 years on Google Trends)
- Product price range $30-150 with 3x markup potential
- At least 3 established competitors but none dominating
- Low legal/restriction risk (not supplements, not fashion, not firearms)

For each niche, give:
1. Specific sub-audience
2. Their top 3 problems or desires
3. 3 example products that fit
4. 3 competing Shopify stores (real ones)
5. One unique differentiation angle
```

Validate every output manually — LLMs confidently invent competitor stores and market data.

## Red flags during research

- You can't name 5 existing stores in the niche → too obscure
- Every store uses the same 3 products → commodity, race to the bottom
- Ad Library shows < 10 total ads across the whole niche → either untested or platforms don't allow it
- Your target customer can't be reached by any single interest on Meta → targeting will be expensive
- You find yourself saying "but mine will be different because of design" → not enough differentiation

## The final test

Write a one-sentence elevator pitch:

> "[Brand] sells [specific products] to [specific person] who wants [specific outcome]."

Example (good):
> "Barrel & Oak sells beard-care products to suburban dads over 40 who want to feel handsome without fussy routines."

Example (bad):
> "We sell home goods for everyone."

If yours looks like the second, go back to Step 1.
